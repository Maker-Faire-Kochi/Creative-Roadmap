02 / Core content engine

Maker
Stories.

Every onboarded maker gets a story system, not a single promotional post. The same raw material can become a Reel, carousel, article, quote card, project walkthrough, event-day cut and post-event profile.

The person

Why do you make?

  • The first thing they built
  • The obsession behind the project
  • What failed
  • What keeps them going
The project

Show me the thing.

  • What it does
  • How it works
  • What is weird / beautiful / useful
  • What changed from version 1
The world

Why should I care?

  • Problem or curiosity
  • Who it is for
  • What they learned
  • What they want to make next
“A maker profile should feel like a story about a human who happens to have made something extraordinary.”
Story angle

“I didn't set out to become a maker.”

Trace the accidental beginning: childhood repair, curiosity, a broken thing, a campus club, a side project, a problem nobody else wanted to solve.

Story angle

“Here's how the impossible became a prototype.”

Make the process visible. Hands, tools, sketches, failed versions, code, materials and the moment the prototype finally works.

Story angle

“The version that didn't work.”

Failure makes maker culture relatable. Show the wrong circuit, broken print, rejected idea or embarrassing prototype — and what changed afterwards.

Story angle

“What I wish more people understood about making.”

Give the maker a point of view. This is where a project becomes a culture story rather than a product advertisement.

Story angle

“If I had one more year…”

End each profile with the next experiment. It creates a natural bridge to the Faire and gives the audience a reason to follow the maker after the event.

One maker → many outputs

Content multiplication

1 interview → 1 hero Reel + 1 long-form article + 3 short clips + 1 carousel + 5 quote cards + 1 project walkthrough + event-day follow-up + post-event story.

Maker publishes? Yes, whenever possible. Co-create the story and give the maker ready-to-post cuts, captions and assets so their own audience becomes an amplification layer.

04 / Editorial calendar

Build the
anticipation.

Cadence is deliberately story-led. The event announcement should not dominate the feed; the growing cast of makers should.

  • Maker Faire 101: “It isn't a conference. It's a place where people show what they make.”
  • Kerala Already Makes: 4–6 stories showing making outside the obvious tech/startup frame.
  • Maker Party Kochi archive: the 2014 story, including its 30 stations and 4,000+ attendees, as a historical signal of community appetite.
  • First Maker Story pilots: 2–3 strong profiles to establish the visual/editorial grammar.
  • 2–3 Maker Stories per week, mixing established and emerging makers.
  • Audience prompts: “Show us what you made”, “Nominate a maker”, “What would you build?”
  • Maker-led posts: ask featured makers to publish their own version / behind-the-scenes story.
  • Community formats: TinkerSpace tour, MakerGram conversation, KSUM/Maker Village ecosystem stories.
  • “Meet the Maker” countdown begins.
  • Every confirmed maker gets a project reveal + human profile + “what to try” hook.
  • Build guides: “What can I actually do at Maker Faire?”
  • Audience utility: schedule, venue, accessibility, family/student guidance, registration and FAQ.
  • Capture 10–20 micro Maker Stories per day: name, project, why, one surprising detail.
  • Publish “maker of the moment”, project demos, reactions and failures.
  • Give makers a same-day asset pack: portrait, 10–20s clip, tag/caption suggestion.
  • Hero recap: “What Kerala made together.”
  • 5–10 deeper Maker Stories: what changed after the Faire.
  • Data story: makers, projects, attendees, workshops, states represented, community connections — use verified final numbers only.
  • “What happens next?” follow-ups to keep the maker network alive.
05 / Maker Story operating system

Make the
content machine.

The team should be able to onboard a maker on Monday and have a complete story package ready for distribution by Friday.

01 / Intake

Maker story form

  • Name / project / location / category
  • What are you presenting?
  • Why did you make it?
  • What failed?
  • What do you want visitors to try?
  • What are you making next?
  • Links, social handles, photo/video assets
02 / Interview

30-minute conversation

  • 5 min: origin
  • 10 min: project + process
  • 5 min: failure / unexpected discovery
  • 5 min: belief about making
  • 5 min: Faire invitation / next build
03 / Capture

Film the hands.

Face, hands, tools, materials, prototype, failure, environment, finished project. Avoid only talking-head footage.

04 / Package

One story, many cuts.

Hero Reel + short clips + carousel + article + quotes + portrait + maker-provided caption + event-day update.

05 / Amplify

Let the maker own it.

Give every maker a ready-to-share kit. Encourage cross-posting, tagging and their own first-person version.

Editorial rule

PEOPLE > ORGANISATIONS.
STORIES > ANNOUNCEMENTS.

Organisations provide context. Makers provide the heartbeat. Do not turn every post into an institutional announcement.

07 / Measurement

Measure the
culture shift.

Do not judge the campaign only by impressions. The ladder should move from recognition to participation to continuity.

01
Reach

Impressions, unique reach, video views, profile visits, search interest.

02
Recognition

Branded search, direct traffic, mentions, “Maker Faire” recall in qualitative feedback.

03
Interaction

Comments, shares, saves, replies, nominations, maker submissions and UGC.

04
Participation

Registrations, Call for Makers applications, workshop sign-ups, volunteer/community joins.

05
Community

Cross-posts, maker-to-maker introductions, new collaborations, repeat contributors.

06
Continuity

February story views, maker follow-through, returning creators and post-event community activity.
